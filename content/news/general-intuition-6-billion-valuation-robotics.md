## The quick version

A startup called General Intuition is reportedly in talks to raise a new round of funding that would value the company at $6 billion — before the new money even comes in. That's nearly three times what it was worth just a few weeks earlier, when it raised $320 million at a $2.3 billion valuation. New backers said to be joining include Valor Equity Partners, Point72 Ventures, and Seven Seven Six, on top of existing investors Khosla Ventures and General Catalyst.

## What does General Intuition actually build?

The company builds "foundation models" — big, general-purpose AI systems — that are meant to teach AI agents and robots how to move through space and time the way humans and animals intuitively do. Think of it as trying to give a robot a sense of physical common sense: how to navigate a room, react to a moving object, or understand cause and effect in the physical world.

The company's approach to training this kind of AI is unusual: it uses hundreds of millions of hours of video game footage. That's not a coincidence — General Intuition's founder, Pim de Witte, previously built Medal, a platform where gamers share clips of their gameplay. That gave the company a massive head start on training data. Specifically, they use "action labels," which are records of exactly which buttons a player pressed and precisely when — essentially a detailed log of cause-and-effect decisions made by a real person in a simulated environment.

## Why gameplay data, and why does it matter?

Investor Vinod Khosla, whose firm backed the company, argues these action labels are key to something AI researchers call "generalization" — the ability for a model to apply what it learned in one situation to a completely different one it's never seen before. The idea is that if an AI learns enough about how actions lead to outcomes in a huge variety of game scenarios, it can start applying that same instinct to new tasks, including ones in the real, physical world — like controlling a robot.

## Where the money is going

The startup says the new funding will go toward three things: improving its core AI model with a specific focus on robotics ("embodiment," in industry jargon, meaning giving AI a physical body to control), buying more computing power (it has a partnership with cloud company CoreWeave for this), and hiring more people.

One detail that stands out: this deal reportedly marks Valor Equity Partners' first investment in an AI lab since it backed SpaceX. That's a signal of how seriously some investors are taking the bet that "AI that understands physical space" could be as big a deal as AI that understands language.
