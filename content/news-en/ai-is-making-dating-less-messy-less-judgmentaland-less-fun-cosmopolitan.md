Where singles used to send screenshots of confusing chat conversations to their friend group, they're now increasingly bringing their romantic doubts to artificial intelligence. Language models such as ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini are being widely used as instantly available, judgment-free dating coaches.

Research from the Kinsey Institute and Match Group shows that AI use in the search for love has risen 300 percent in a year. More than a quarter of singles between 18 and 39 now use AI tools while dating, though nearly half of that group is well aware of the downsides beforehand.

## The promise of neutral feedback

For many users, AI's appeal lies in its perceived objectivity. Friends often give advice based on their own experiences and emotional involvement. A chatbot dissects messages, patterns, and dating profiles without that personal baggage.

Relationship therapists and coaches, however, see a clear downside. AI is entirely dependent on the information the user provides. Someone asking questions out of dating anxiety or insecurity risks having the algorithm confirm those very fears. A language model also cannot read body language, tone of voice, or subtle human nuance, meaning the insights it provides often remain confined to a digital echo chamber.

## From text correction to recording during the date

Real-world applications range from rewriting Hinge replies to more drastic methods. Some users deploy smart glasses like Meta Glasses or voice recorders to record their dates and have AI analyze them afterward for compatibility. This practice raises major ethical and legal questions when it happens without the other person's consent.

Others try to fully automate the matching process by training AI models to score profiles against specific criteria. While the urge for efficiency is understandable within a dating culture that can feel exhausting, this pragmatic approach often backfires once there's actually a real person sitting across from you.

Experts emphasize that love and relationships cannot be "hacked" like a business project. The search for a suitable partner requires spontaneity, vulnerability, and the willingness to take risks — elements that fade into the background amid constant overanalysis of data. AI can help recognize behavioral patterns, but the essence of human attraction remains unpredictable.
