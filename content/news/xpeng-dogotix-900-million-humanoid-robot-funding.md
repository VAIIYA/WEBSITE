## The quick version

XPeng, the Chinese electric car maker, just spun its humanoid robot ambitions into a dedicated subsidiary called Dogotix — and that subsidiary has raised over $900 million in its first funding round. The deal values Dogotix at more than $6.3 billion after the raise, which reportedly makes it the largest private financing deal China's "embodied AI" sector has seen. Dogotix is led by XPeng's own CEO, He Xiaopeng.

## What happened

"Embodied AI" is the industry's term for artificial intelligence that has to control a physical body — sensors, joints, motors — rather than just answering questions in a chat window. Dogotix is building a humanoid robot called IRON, and the plan is aggressive: 1,000 units a month by the end of 2026, with paying customers getting deliveries starting in 2027, initially in retail stores and industrial campuses.

The pitch to investors leans hard on XPeng's day job. Building electric cars already requires precision motors, layered sensor systems, onboard computers, and self-driving software — all things a walking, working robot also needs. XPeng is betting that the manufacturing muscle it built scaling up car production translates directly into scaling up robot production, which is usually the hardest part for robotics startups that have never mass-produced anything before.

## Why it matters

This isn't happening in isolation. Humanoid robotics has pulled in more than $8.7 billion in venture funding this year alone, and Chinese companies have captured roughly two-thirds of that money. XPeng joins a crowded field of Chinese automakers and tech firms racing to be the ones who figure out mass-produced humanoids first, treating it as the natural next platform after electric vehicles and autonomous driving.

A car company turning into a robot company also says something about where the money sees the ceiling: a assembly line worker or retail associate is a much bigger addressable market than another EV buyer in an already saturated market.

## What's next

The real test isn't the funding round, it's whether Dogotix can actually hit that 1,000-units-a-month target without the reliability and safety issues that have plagued other humanoid robot demos. If IRON ships on schedule into real retail and industrial settings in 2027, it becomes one of the first humanoid robots most people encounter directly rather than watch in a promotional video.
