The U.S. Department of Defense was forced to issue a public denial on Monday after President Donald Trump published a realistic AI-generated video depicting a fictitious attack on Iran’s primary petroleum export hub.

The synthetic video, shared to Trump’s official Truth Social account, showed oil storage tanks and refining infrastructure engulfed in flames from the perspective of an observer inside a helicopter. The post included text claiming that Kharg Island was "being blown to smithereens," signed "President DJT."

## Official Denials and Iranian Response

A U.S. military official confirmed that no military strikes had been conducted against Kharg Island. Hamid Bovard, head of the National Iranian Oil Company, characterized the president's post as "laughable" and confirmed that operations on the island remained calm and unaffected.

The incident underscores the risks associated with hyper-realistic AI media during active military conflicts. The White House has previously affirmed that Trump’s posts on Truth Social constitute official statements from the commander in chief.

## Military Reality Versus Synthetic Content

While the footage of Kharg Island was fabricated, real-world military engagements continue in the region as the conflict enters its seventh month. U.S. officials confirmed targeted operations near Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz, where Iranian forces were allegedly preparing to deploy mine-laying rockets to obstruct maritime traffic. Additionally, U.S. forces intercepted Iranian missiles directed at Jordan.

In an interview with Fox News, Trump defended the broader war effort, claiming the administration was achieving its objectives despite the absence of a formal cease-fire or signed agreement. 

## Digital Warfare and Economic Pressures

The deliberate deployment of synthetic video content by a head of state marks a notable evolution in digital media during wartime, presenting new challenges for official military communications and verification processes.

Alongside military operations, the administration has recently expanded its strategy against Tehran. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth stated on Monday that while kinetic strikes remain on the table, economic measures designed to restrict Iranian state revenue currently take precedence.
