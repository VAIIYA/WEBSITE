## The quick version

An anonymous leaker going by "CyberLeek" has spent the past couple of weeks trickling out unreleased Grand Theft Auto VI material, and the latest wave — posted this week — includes gameplay clips of protagonist Jason Duval and what's claimed to be a complete overhead map of the game's fictional setting, Leonida. Rockstar has been issuing takedowns as fast as it can, but new clips keep surfacing anyway, and the timing puts extra pressure on Rockstar right as its own official Netflix trailer is due in a couple of days.

## What happened

CyberLeek appears to have gotten hold of an actual working build of the game — not just leaked concept art or a stolen script, but a version of GTA VI you could theoretically load up and play, complete with in-progress physics and animations. Recent clips show Duval jumping around a waterfront property, shooting hoops on a driveway basketball hoop (each basket ticks up an in-game "focus" meter), and later crashing a car before getting into a street fight with a delivery driver. In an earlier clip, the leaker used in-game gunfire to spray the word "LEEK" onto a wall — a taunt aimed squarely at Rockstar, and a way of proving the footage is both fresh and from their hands.

The leaked map image labels five in-game regions — Lummox, Kelly, Leonard, Vice-Dale, and Mariana — though a fan mapping project that's been piecing together GTA VI's world for years has pushed back on whether the image is genuine. The leaker has framed the whole campaign as a protest against Rockstar's decision not to sell a physical disc version of the game, rather than a purely mercenary hack-and-dump.

## Why it matters

For Rockstar, this is close to a worst-case scenario for a game this anticipated: a steady drip of real, playable footage undercutting the studio's own marketing rollout, clip by clip, for weeks. It also complicates the polished narrative Rockstar wants to control ahead of launch — every leak forces the studio to react rather than dictate the story, and gives players a preview built from raw, unpolished builds rather than curated trailers.

## What's next

Rockstar has a Netflix-exclusive trailer reportedly locked in for August 27, just two days out, which is likely why the studio hasn't been able to get ahead of this with an early official reveal of its own. Whether CyberLeek keeps posting before or after that trailer drops will shape how much of the conversation around GTA VI's next big marketing beat gets hijacked by leaked footage instead of the real thing.
