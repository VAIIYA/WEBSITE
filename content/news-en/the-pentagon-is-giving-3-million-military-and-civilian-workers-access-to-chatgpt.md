The U.S. Department of War is expanding its internal artificial intelligence infrastructure, granting approximately three million military service members and civilian personnel access to customized versions of OpenAI’s ChatGPT and SpaceXAI’s Grok. Both language models have been integrated into `GenAI.mil`, the custom platform launched by the Pentagon late last year.

The rollout follows defense contracts valued at up to $200 million each awarded to OpenAI and SpaceXAI. Under the deployment parameters, the tailored services—`ChatGPT Mil` and `Starshield AI’s Grok for Government`—are approved to process Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) up to Impact Level 5 (IL5). The Pentagon confirmed that data processed within these environments will remain completely isolated and will not be used to train commercial base models.

## Streamlining Administrative and Logistic Workflows

Despite the military context, the primary purpose of adding these generative models is to streamline daily administrative and operational tasks. The vast majority of defense workloads involve document-heavy processes such as logistics, human resources management, acquisition market research, and contracting. 

The integration supports an ongoing push by War Secretary Pete Hegseth to build an "AI-first" operational environment. The directive aligns with broader White House policy urging rapid integration of commercial artificial intelligence technologies across the armed forces to preserve strategic advantage.

## Evolving Ecosystem and Vendor Friction

The `GenAI.mil` portal initially launched in December offering access exclusively to a military-adapted version of Google's Gemini model. The Department of War reports that the platform has already onboarded roughly 1.7 million active users.

Noticeably absent from the expanded lineup is Anthropic’s Claude model. The exclusion stems from a high-profile disagreement between defense leadership and Anthropic over acceptable-use policies concerning autonomous weapons systems and surveillance. While the conflict led to formal regulatory restrictions on the vendor earlier this year, legal challenges regarding Anthropic's status in defense procurement remain ongoing.
