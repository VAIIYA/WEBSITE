Major music publishers including Sony Music, EMI, and Warner Chappell have filed a copyright infringement lawsuit against Anthropic. The suit alleges that the AI developer illegally torrented and scraped thousands of copyrighted songbooks, sheet music collections, and lyrics to build and refine its Claude AI models.

The legal action follows Anthropic's recent $1.5 billion settlement with book authors over pirated training material. According to the music publishers, that settlement is insufficient to deter a company now valued at $2 trillion from engaging in unauthorized data acquisition.

## Internal Chats Reveal Piracy Directives

To support their claims, the plaintiffs unsealed internal communications showing how Anthropic gathered training data in its early stages. The complaint alleges that in July 2021, co-founder Benjamin Mann personally used BitTorrent to download millions of pirated works from Library Genesis (LibGen) with approval from CEO Dario Amodei.

When law enforcement shut down LibGen, Anthropic allegedly turned to the Pirate Library Mirror (PiLiMi) to access content from Z-Library. Internal messages cited in the filing show Mann telling team members that a mirror drop arrived "just in time," to which an employee responded, "zlibrary my beloved." Publishers argue these exchanges demonstrate a corporate culture that actively relied on piracy to accelerate model development.

## Synthetic Data and Fair Use Defense

While Anthropic maintains that its commercial Claude models are not trained directly on torrented books, publishers argue this distinction is misleading. The complaint asserts that non-commercial models trained on LibGen and PiLiMi datasets were used to generate "synthetic data" and provide reinforced feedback to fine-tune commercial Claude releases.

Additionally, unsealed documents indicate that Anthropic utilized the pirated library to build guardrails, checking whether model outputs matched copyrighted source text too closely.

An Anthropic spokesperson dismissed the lawsuit, stating that the plaintiffs are recycling old allegations and reiterating that training generative AI models constitutes transformative fair use under existing legal precedent.

## Market Harm and Industry Impact

Unlike previous litigation involving book authors, music rights holders argue they can demonstrate direct market harm. The complaint points out that Claude can generate verbatim lyrics, produce stylistic imitations, or combine original lyrics with AI-generated text. Publishers contend that AI-generated songs resulting from this training compete directly with human songwriters on music charts, diluting royalty pools.

The plaintiffs are asking the court for an injunction and a full accounting of Anthropic’s training datasets and methodologies, arguing that unauthorized data usage undermines the financial viability of music creators.
