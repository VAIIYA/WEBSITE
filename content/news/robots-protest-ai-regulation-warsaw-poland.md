## The quick version

On Monday, roughly 30 robots — walking humanoids and dog-like four-legged machines — were paraded in front of the Polish digital affairs ministry in Warsaw. Loudspeakers played chants like "Defend workplaces," "Time for rules," and "Don't wait, regulate." It was a publicity stunt with a serious point: the organizers want stronger laws before AI and robots start replacing large numbers of workers.

## What happened

The demonstration was organized by Democratism, a group that says it wants to start a public debate about how automation affects jobs. Obviously the robots weren't protesting on their own — people operated and staged them — but the imagery was the message.

Organizer Grzegorz Kulis told AFP the concern is humanoid robots and AI replacing people in "cognitive work," not just manual labor: "If we don't react now, we may soon have a problem." Poland's Digital Affairs Minister Krzysztof Gawkowski showed up and spoke with Kulis at the scene.

The timing isn't random. Poland is in the middle of implementing the European Union's AI Act, the bloc's rulebook for how AI can be built and used.

## Why it matters

It is a small, theatrical event, but it reflects a real anxiety that is showing up across Europe: that the rules for AI in the workplace are being written after the technology is already being deployed. Getting a government minister to engage on the street is a sign the jobs question is politically live.

## What's next

The EU AI Act's provisions continue phasing in across member states, including Poland. Expect more campaigning from labor and civil-society groups pushing for worker protections specifically tied to automation, separate from the AI Act's broader safety focus.
