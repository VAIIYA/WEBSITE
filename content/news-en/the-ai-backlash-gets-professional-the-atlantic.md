Public skepticism toward artificial intelligence is reaching a critical inflection point in the United States. A new advocacy organization named Irreplaceable has formally launched, uniting experienced organizers from the climate movement to challenge the rapid, largely unregulated expansion of AI technologies and their infrastructure.

Led by veterans of organizations such as 350.org and the Sunrise Movement, Irreplaceable aims to channel widespread public anxiety over job security, mass surveillance, and resource consumption into organized political action. Rather than relying primarily on policy white papers, the group is deploying the populist, emotion-driven strategies that defined recent climate activism.

## Adapting the Climate Playbook

Irreplaceable’s leadership argues that early climate efforts faltered by relying too heavily on technical arguments aimed at lawmakers rather than grassroots organizing. By contrast, the new coalition seeks to direct existing public frustration straight at tech executives and regulators.

The group’s organizers have already begun coordinating high-profile demonstrations, including a recent march in San Francisco that protested outside the offices of OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google. Strategist Jeremy Ornstein and executive director Phil Aroneanu contend that intuitive public concerns about losing control to powerful machine systems can be converted into sustained political leverage.

## Environmental Coalitions and Local Resistance

The movement is building concrete alliances with established environmental groups, including Earthjustice, the Sierra Club, and the NAACP’s environmental justice arm. These organizations are fighting data center construction across several states, pointing to heavy grid demands, fossil-fuel consumption, and rising local utility costs.

This localized strategy is showing early results. Dozens of proposed data centers have faced delays or cancellations due to public pushback, and candidates in recent state primaries have successfully campaigned on opposition to tech infrastructure expansion.

## Strategic Challenges and Market Speed

Despite early visibility, Irreplaceable faces structural challenges. The movement currently focuses on unified opposition rather than presenting a comprehensive policy alternative comparable to the climate movement's Green New Deal framework. Irreplaceable plans to build out its coalition over the coming year, aiming to codify and pass federal policy guardrails by 2029.

That multi-year timeline risks lagging behind the tech sector's rapid deployment schedule and substantial political spending. Irreplaceable is ultimately betting that broad public backlash can organize quickly enough to force democratic oversight onto an industry currently moving at unprecedented speed.
