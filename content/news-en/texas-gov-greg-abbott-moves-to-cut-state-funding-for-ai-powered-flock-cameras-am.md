Texas Governor Greg Abbott has directed state agencies to halt funding for Flock Safety, an artificial intelligence-driven automated license plate reader platform, following mounting public concern over surveillance expansion and data privacy.

While municipal law enforcement departments operate most Flock systems using local or federal funds, Texas state agencies will no longer be permitted to allocate state grant dollars toward the software or hardware. The executive action follows decisions by several Texas municipalities—including the city of Pflugerville and Hood County—to cancel their contracts and disconnect active cameras.

## Advanced Vehicle Tracking Technology

Flock Safety has established itself as one of the prominent vendors of automated license plate readers (ALPRs) in the United States. Beyond logging license plate numbers, the company’s computer vision models analyze camera footage to identify distinct vehicle features, including make, model, color, unique dents, and bumper stickers.

Law enforcement agencies rely on the platform to search vehicle data across connected municipal networks. Advocates contend the real-time alerts assist officers in solving violent crimes, recovering stolen automobiles, and locating missing individuals. 

However, civil liberties advocates and privacy organizations warn that building a broad, interconnected tracking network threatens public privacy and risks operational abuse. In Texas, the Motor Vehicle Crime Prevention Authority had assisted state and local entities in installing over 3,000 Flock cameras since 2023, utilizing funds derived from a $1 fee on auto insurance policies originally intended to address catalytic converter theft.

## National Scrutiny Amplifies

The regulatory shift in Texas aligns with broader pushback at both state and federal levels. US Senator Josh Hawley (R-Mo.) recently initiated an inquiry into Flock Safety, highlighting that the company's network comprises more than 120,000 cameras spanning 49 states, capturing upwards of 20 billion vehicle scans monthly.

Addressing the growing criticism, Flock Safety spokesperson Paris Lewbel emphasized the platform's role in public safety while calling for clear regulatory boundaries. Lewbel stated the company supports strong privacy standards, transparency, and accountability guardrails to ensure the technology is deployed responsibly without hindering legitimate police operations.
