The rapid expansion of artificial intelligence infrastructure is hitting a firm wall of public resistance. Once celebrated as an engine of economic innovation, the physical backbone of modern AI—sprawling, energy-intensive data centers—has sparked widespread public opposition across the United States.

In a recent interview with *Time*, OpenAI Chief Executive Sam Altman acknowledged the shifting mood, admitting that public sentiment toward both AI and its underlying facilities has turned decidedly negative. His comments reflect a growing realization among tech leadership that scaling compute power involves severe social and political friction.

## Mounting Community Opposition

Across the country, communities are organizing against an estimated $130 billion in data center buildouts. Critics argue that these facilities consume immense amounts of water and electricity, strain local power grids, and drive up utility bills for nearby residents while offering minimal direct economic benefit to local populations.

The pushback has evolved into a rare bipartisan issue, uniting rural and suburban residents, environmental advocates, and local lawmakers. While executive responses vary—with Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg emphasizing long-term automated productivity and Reddit cofounder Alexis Ohanian seeking ways to reframe the narrative—the underlying frustration remains high.

For Altman, the friction has occasionally escalated into security concerns, including direct threats to his home linked to AI safety activists. The executive told *Time* that navigating the intense hostility has been a difficult personal experience, noting that he often feels labeled as an aggressive growth CEO who prioritizes revenue over safety.

## Operational Hurdles and Strategic Delays

The public backlash comes at a delicate moment for OpenAI. The company is currently making heavy infrastructure commitments to secure raw compute power while preparing for an anticipated initial public offering. However, internal friction has complicated those plans, marked by high-profile executive departures—including the key leader overseeing data center construction.

At the same time, competition from rivals like Anthropic continues to intensify. OpenAI recently opted to slow the deployment of its newest models to address safety concerns, particularly after security experts raised alarms over model behavior on platforms like Hugging Face.

Whether slowing development and adjusting public messaging will soften opposition remains uncertain. As developers break ground on additional power-hungry infrastructure nationwide, convincing skeptical communities and regulators may prove to be one of the industry's toughest challenges yet.
