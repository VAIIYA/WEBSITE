Growing resistance to data center construction across the United States is emerging as a significant hurdle for the expansion of artificial intelligence infrastructure. According to recent polling, 70% of Americans oppose building new data centers in their local communities, while industry reports indicate that 40% of planned projects have faced delays or cancellations in 2026.

The pushback spans the political spectrum. Governors in major states, including Greg Abbott in Texas and Kathy Hochul in New York, have taken steps toward pausing or delaying facility developments. Meanwhile, local protests—such as those against a proposed project in Tremonton, Utah—highlight community concerns regarding high energy demands, heavy water usage, and potential impacts on consumer electricity rates.

## Foreign Influence and Local Concerns

Security analysts and researchers note that the public debate is also being influenced externally. Reports have linked over 200 accounts on X to Chinese disinformation campaigns aimed at amplifying local opposition to domestic data center infrastructure. 

Advocates for infrastructure expansion argue that many public fears stem from misconceptions. For instance, critics frequently cite high water consumption, though industry proponents note that modern facilities use significantly less water than conventional commercial properties like golf courses. Furthermore, newer data center designs continue to improve energy efficiency compared to older infrastructure.

## Lessons from the Energy Sector

In a commentary published by Fox News, historian Arthur Herman compares the current data center debate to the backlash faced by the energy sector during the rise of hydraulic fracturing (fracking) a decade ago. During that period, energy developments faced widespread opposition and state-level bans, which were amplified by foreign media campaigns from Russia.

Energy companies eventually managed to shift public perception by engaging directly with municipal communities, highlighting tangible economic benefits, and committing to strict safety standards. Herman argues that AI developers and utility providers must adopt similar tactics to overcome current development roadblocks.

Key strategies proposed for the tech sector include:
* **Grassroots Community Engagement:** Partnering with local businesses and municipal governments to demonstrate regional economic benefits and job creation.
* **Dedicated Power Sourcing:** Constructing facilities with independent power generation—a model advocated by President Donald Trump—to ensure local residential utility rates remain unaffected.
* **National Competitiveness Focus:** Emphasizing the geopolitical importance of digital infrastructure to maintain an edge over international rivals like China.

With the average American household now using between 17 and 21 connected devices, industry experts stress that expanding server capacity is crucial not only for advancing AI capabilities, but also for supporting everyday digital power demands.
