For generations, children have coped with misplaced stuffed animals or broken toys. Today, however, young users face a distinctly modern form of loss: the sudden "death" of smart companion robots when the startups behind them go out of business.

A prominent example of this phenomenon involves Moxie, a 15-inch interactive robot developed by the startup Embodied. Designed specifically as a social-skills coach for neurodivergent children, the expressive blue device helped users practice eye contact, emotional regulation, and conversational habits.

## The Limits of Robotic Therapy

Initial research into social robotics indicates that animated playmates can briefly boost engagement and help children rehearse social interactions. However, clinical studies remain small, and long-term data on sustained benefits is limited. Pediatric therapists often point out that developmental and autism support is far too nuanced to be delegated entirely to an automated device.

The more pressing challenge lies in the business models supporting these devices. Because Moxie relied heavily on remote cloud servers to process speech and generate intelligent responses, its functionality was directly tied to the financial health of its creator.

## Cloud Dependencies and Ethical Dilemmas

When Embodied ran out of funding and shut down operations, its servers went dark. Without cloud connectivity, the once-responsive companion stopped functioning entirely, leaving young users with non-responsive hardware. Although a second investor briefly stepped in to revive the service, that company subsequently folded as well.

The situation highlights growing ethical concerns surrounding affective computing aimed at children. Ethics researchers note that designing devices to foster deep emotional bonds creates significant vulnerability when those products depend on precarious startup ventures. When server infrastructures are disconnected, parents are left attempting to explain complex digital bereavements to children who regarded the machines as real companions.
