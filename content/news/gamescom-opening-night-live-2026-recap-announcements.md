## The quick version

Gamescom 2026's Opening Night Live wrapped up its two-hour showcase in Cologne, and the headline moment was CD Projekt Red's first proper look at The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt — Songs of the Past. Alongside that, the show delivered fresh trailers for Final Fantasy VII Revelation, Gears of War: E-Day, and Game of Thrones: War for Westeros, plus a batch of smaller reveals that filled out the rest of the runtime.

## What was shown

Songs of the Past turned out to be bigger than a typical add-on — CD Projekt Red is explicitly calling it a full "expansion" rather than DLC, comparing its scope to Blood and Wine, one of the original Witcher 3's most acclaimed story add-ons. It's being built in partnership with Fools Theory, the studio also handling the ground-up remake of the first Witcher game, and it's due in 2027 across PC, PS5, and Xbox Series X/S. CD Projekt also confirmed Gwent, the card minigame fans have been asking to see return, will be part of it. In the same breath, the studio reconfirmed The Witcher 4 is still tracking for a 2028 window.

Beyond the Witcher news, the show packed in a wide spread of genres: Nodus Fall, a new co-op action game from Genshin Impact studio Hoyoverse; Worms: Galactic Tactics, landing in 2027; an expanded Onimusha: Way of the Sword demo ahead of its September 4 release; a sequel to strategy game Humankind; and Mariachi Legends, a Metroidvania mixing Castlevania-style exploration with Mexican folklore and music, launching April 2027.

## Why it matters

Opening Night Live has become one of the three or four nights a year the entire games industry synchronizes its biggest reveals around, alongside Keighley's Summer Game Fest and The Game Awards. For a franchise as dormant-feeling as The Witcher 3 — a nine-year-old game — getting a full-scope expansion rather than a nostalgia re-release signals CD Projekt Red still sees real commercial upside in the base game while The Witcher 4 finishes development. For smaller titles like Mariachi Legends or Nodus Fall, a slot in a marquee show like this is often the single biggest visibility moment they'll get before launch.

## What's next

Gamescom's show floor stays open in Cologne for several more days, where journalists and attendees get hands-on time with several of the games just revealed. Expect a wave of previews and developer interviews digging into Songs of the Past's Gwent integration and story scope specifically, since that's the announcement with the largest built-in fanbase already primed to react.
