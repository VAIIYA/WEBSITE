Elon Musk made striking comments about the future and safety of artificial intelligence during an internal meeting. According to sources at tech outlet *The Information*, Musk addressed employees of Cursor, the developer studio acquired by SpaceX for $60 billion, for the first time.

During the meeting, Musk stated that it's a foregone conclusion that AI models will one day become so advanced that humans will no longer be able to control them. His conclusion from that premise is striking: precisely because of that, he believes SpaceX's AI division should do everything it can to develop the technology faster than the competition.

## Logical contradiction

The CEO's reasoning raises real questions within the tech industry. If it's truly inevitable that artificial intelligence will slip beyond human control, it's hard to explain why winning the development race would make the end result safer.

Unlike a deterrence-style logic — where possessing a technology prevents an opponent from using it — building an uncontrollable system faster simply means reaching an uncontrollable situation faster. Musk did not explain why his companies would be capable of guaranteeing a safe outcome, and instead mainly emphasized the need to finish first.

## Concerns about safety and policy

This stance is at odds with the approach taken by competitors such as Anthropic, which claim to develop their AI models with an explicit focus on safety margins and risk management. Musk's remarks are once again fueling the debate over how tech industry leaders handle potentially disruptive technologies.

Musk's earlier projects in artificial intelligence have also regularly come under scrutiny. The Grok chatbot, developed by his company xAI, has faced heavy criticism over poor moderation. The system has made headlines for generating hateful content, conspiracy theories, and being involved in the generation of non-consensual deepfakes.

The talk at Cursor underscores Musk's vision of the global AI race. Where ethicists and policymakers call for caution and stricter frameworks as AI models grow more powerful, Musk is choosing maximum acceleration — even as he expects humanity to ultimately lose control.
