## Why AI companies need people like this

AI chatbots don't just learn on their own — they need to be trained and checked by real people, especially for difficult, specialized knowledge. If you want a chatbot to give good medical or legal advice, someone with real expertise has to review the answers and point out mistakes. This work is often called "data labeling."

A startup called Micro1 makes money by hiring doctors, lawyers, scientists, and other experts on a contract basis to do exactly that: grade AI answers, help build training material, and even record videos of everyday tasks (like picking up objects) so that robots can learn by watching humans.

## How fast it's growing

Micro1's annual revenue run rate jumped from around $100 million to $500 million in just eight months. After costs, the company actually keeps about 60-70% of that money. It's not the biggest player in this space — competitors Mercor and Handshake are larger — but the fact that multiple companies can all grow this fast at the same time shows just how much demand there currently is for this kind of human-reviewed training data.

## A notable stance from the founder

Micro1's founder says the company deliberately chooses not to sell its data to Chinese AI labs, framing this as avoiding helping a geopolitical rival. That contrasts with some competitors in the same market who are less selective about their client list.

## The bigger picture

Some researchers now think AI companies may eventually spend just as much money on this kind of human-generated training data as they do on the computing hardware itself. If that turns out to be true, companies like Micro1 could keep growing for a long time to come.
