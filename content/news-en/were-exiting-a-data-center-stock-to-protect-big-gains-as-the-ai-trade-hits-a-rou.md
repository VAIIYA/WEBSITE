Jim Cramer’s Charitable Trust has fully exited its position in optical fiber and materials supplier Corning, selling off its final 395 shares at approximately $146 each. The move marks a strategic effort to lock in profits and reduce exposure to artificial intelligence infrastructure stocks amid a shifting market mood.

The sale completes a successful run for the Trust, securing an average gain of 52% on shares accumulated between October 2025 and August 2026. While Corning has dropped significantly from its peak close of $255 in June, the stock remains up 67% year-to-date—far outperforming the S&P 500’s roughly 12% return over the same period.

## A Muted Response to Strong AI Signals

The decision to de-risk comes as Wall Street displays growing fatigue toward AI hardware and data center suppliers. The Trust highlighted a growing disconnect between strong corporate results and stagnant stock reactions across the broader AI ecosystem.

A key indicator of this shift was Nvidia’s recent financial report. The chipmaker delivered stellar numbers and issued fiscal 2028 revenue growth guidance of 70% year-over-year—well above Wall Street consensus estimates of 45%. Under typical market conditions, such projections would spark a broad sector rally. Instead, the market largely shrugged off the news, indicating that investors are becoming far more hesitant about the valuation multiples assigned to the AI buildout.

## Pivoting to a Defensive Posture

Rather than risking further profit erosion, the portfolio team opted to take money off the table. Corning has been placed on the Trust’s "Bullpen" watchlist, allowing analysts to monitor the stock for potential re-entry if valuation multiples compress to more attractive levels or if fears surrounding AI infrastructure spending prove overblown.

Market participants are now turning their attention to upcoming financial disclosures from other enterprise and semiconductor players, including Dell and Broadcom, to gauge whether negative sector sentiment will persist or reverse course in the coming days.
