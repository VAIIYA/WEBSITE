Serial entrepreneur Timothy Armoo is encouraging Gen Z job seekers to shift their focus from an uncertain corporate job market toward building their own technology-driven ventures.

As artificial intelligence increasingly automates entry-level roles across major industries, Armoo argues that the technology simultaneously offers an unprecedented advantage for independent founders. The 31-year-old multimillionaire describes the current era as the most accessible period in history for wealth creation, urging young individuals to leverage generative tools like ChatGPT and Claude to launch small-scale projects.

## Lowering Barriers to Entry

Armoo's perspective stems from his own experience navigating early adversity. Raised partly in Ghana before moving to a London council estate, he launched his first tutoring venture at age 14. He later founded the influencer marketing agency Fanbytes in 2017, which was acquired by Brainlabs in 2022 for an eight-figure sum when he was 27.

To support the next generation of builders, Armoo recently established the Legon Fund, committing £5 million (approximately $6.7 million) of his personal capital to finance AI startups launched by minority founders. He emphasizes that free or low-cost AI platforms, combined with organic social media distribution, eliminate many of the financial and technical obstacles that historically hindered early-stage business development.

## A Generational Shift Toward Self-Employment

Armoo’s outlook aligns with broader workplace trends among younger demographics. Facing sluggish corporate hiring at the entry level, Gen Z workers are increasingly pursuing independent business models over traditional corporate career ladders.

Data from Intuit’s Entrepreneurship in 2026 report indicates that 43% of Gen Z individuals are considering starting a business this year, representing the highest proportion of any age group. Additionally, LinkedIn platform data shows that "founder" has become the second-fastest-growing job title among Gen Z professionals.

Modern digital infrastructure—ranging from website builders to direct-to-consumer video channels—has significantly reduced operational overhead. Other young business founders, such as Chase Gallagher of CMG Landscaping and Steven Schwartz of creator platform Whop, note that accessible mobile and AI tools have democratized client acquisition and business management.

For Armoo, the key takeaway for aspiring entrepreneurs is straightforward: rather than waiting for traditional corporate hiring to rebound, young professionals possess the tools to build and scale independent ventures immediately.
