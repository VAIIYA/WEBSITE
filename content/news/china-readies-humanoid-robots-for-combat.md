## The quick version

Reuters went through more than 100 Chinese military procurement notices, patents, academic papers, and defense-company materials. The picture: China's military is actively researching how humanoid robots — the two-legged, human-shaped kind — could be used in war, and planning for their eventual deployment. No armed robot has been fielded with a real unit yet, and the machines are still power-hungry and unreliable outside demos.

## What happened

The report draws a deliberate contrast. In August, China hosted the World Humanoid Robot Games, where humanoids danced, ran, and competed for entertainment. Two days after it ended, the PLA Daily — the official military newspaper — called on researchers to speed up technology for robotic "combatants."

Behind the scenes, Reuters found military institutions buying robots and training data, testing whether humanoids can meet battlefield requirements, and studying how robots might operate alongside human soldiers. The effort picked up through 2025 and 2026, focused on three hard problems: perception (letting the robot understand its surroundings), manipulation (using hands and tools), and gathering enough training data.

China's commercial robot industry gives the military a big head start. Chinese manufacturers made up roughly 95% of global humanoid robot shipments in 2025, so the PLA can tap a fast-growing industry while building its own military versions.

## Why it matters

Humanoid robots are usually pitched as future factory workers or home helpers. This is a reminder that the same hardware has obvious military interest, and that the country leading humanoid manufacturing is openly exploring it. It also raises the familiar autonomous-weapons questions about how much decision-making a machine should have in a fight.

## What's next

Reuters found no evidence of an armed humanoid deployed with an operational PLA unit, and battery life and reliability remain real limits. Expect continued research, more demonstrations, and growing international debate about rules for autonomous and semi-autonomous weapons.
