Across commercial infrastructure projects in Nevada and Texas, massive excavators are moving thousands of cubic yards of earth with nobody sitting in the cab. San Francisco-based Bedrock Robotics has officially transitioned its autonomous heavy machinery from testing environments to active commercial job sites.

The company's initial deployments focus on high-volume, early-stage site preparation, including rough earthmoving, foundation digging, and cut-and-fill operations. Current projects include work on a Nevada water treatment plant alongside Sundt Construction, as well as multi-million-cubic-yard earthwork developments in Texas with Champion Site Prep and Zachry Construction Corporation.

## How the Bedrock Operator Works

At the core of the deployment is the "Bedrock Operator," a hardware and software system engineered to retrofit standard heavy construction equipment. The platform incorporates a suit of sensors and onboard computing modules designed for rapid single-day installation without permanent machine modifications. 

The software utilizes machine-learning algorithms trained on tens of thousands of field operation hours. Once a project blueprint is uploaded, the autonomous excavator calculates optimal digging paths and handles terrain assessment independently. While Bedrock eventually plans to sell retrofit kits directly to contractors, current field operations utilize custom-configured excavators deployed directly from Bedrock’s facilities.

Safety mechanisms are integrated into the machine’s sensor array. If site personnel or unexpected obstacles enter the equipment's operational radius, the machine halts automatically until the perimeter is clear. Remote fleet operators monitor performance off-site and can step in if an excavator encounters an unresolvable edge case, though Bedrock reports such interventions remain rare.

## Addressing Construction Labor Deficits

Bedrock’s commercial expansion comes as the heavy construction sector faces severe structural labor shortages. Industry statistics indicate that over 40% of the current construction workforce is projected to retire within five years, while contractors continuously report difficulty filling open skilled positions.

By automating repetitive production digging—which can take operators years to master—Bedrock aims to allow experienced human workers to transition into supervisory roles. Company representatives report that productivity levels for their autonomous excavators are already approaching human benchmarks in volume moved per shift.

Founded by former Waymo engineers, Bedrock Robotics has raised over $350 million to scale its technology. The company's broader roadmap includes developing multi-machine coordination, where autonomous excavators, bulldozers, and haul trucks operate as an interconnected fleet on complex job sites.
