## What "vibe-coding" means here

You've probably heard of typing a prompt into an AI chatbot and getting an essay or an image back. "Vibe-coding" applies that same basic idea to software: you roughly describe what you want, and the AI writes the actual code behind the scenes, so you don't need any programming knowledge yourself. Meta's new app, Pocket, applies this specifically to small, playable games.

## How Pocket works

Within Pocket, you can create small interactive games (Meta calls them "gizmos") by simply describing what you want. These small games can respond to touch, react when you tilt your phone, include sound effects, and even use your own photos or your phone's camera in real time. Once you've made one, it shows up in a scrollable feed — similar to scrolling through short videos — where other users can play it, save it, or "remix" it into their own new version.

## Where this comes from

Pocket grew out of Meta's earlier acquisition of a team called Gizmo, and quietly launched last month first in Brazil before rolling out this week to everyone in the United States. Meta is now shutting down the original standalone Gizmo app now that Pocket has taken over.

## Why Meta is betting on this

Meta's CEO has publicly emphasized how much faster the company can now ship new apps, crediting AI-assisted development, which lets small teams launch complete products — Pocket fits into a recent wave of similarly fast releases, including a photo-sharing app, a Reddit-style discussion app, and a marketplace app. The bet is that if ordinary people can make their own small games as easily as writing a text message, a lot more casual, personal content will get made and shared.
